How It Began

The Origin of CJP

A satirical youth movement born from a judge's insult, ignited by a leaked exam, and transformed into the largest student protest in a generation.

The Spark

In May 2026, Chief Justice of India Surya Kant called unemployed youth turning to journalism and activism "cockroaches" and "parasites of society." The remark went viral. Digital strategist Abhijeet Dipke, a Boston University student, posted: "What if all cockroaches come together?" Millions responded.

The Trigger

The 2026 NEET-UG medical entrance exam paper was leaked, forcing cancellation. Re-exams were held, but trust was shattered. ~20 students died by suicide after the leak. Families had taken loans for coaching. Dreams were destroyed. The government refused to take accountability.

"There are already parasites of society who attack the system... There are youngsters like cockroaches; they don't get any employment, and they don't have any place in a profession."
— Chief Justice Surya Kant (May 2026)
"What if all cockroaches come together?"
— Abhijeet Dipke, CJP Founder (16 May 2026)

The Contested Question

Who Started the Violence?

Both sides have a narrative. The evidence is genuinely contested. Here is what the reporting actually shows, without picking a side.

Narrative A

Delhi Police: "Protesters Started It"

  • Protesters attempted to breach barricades near Parliament Street at ~11:25 AM
  • Stone pelting by CJP protesters triggered the lathi charge (police sources)
  • Protesters displayed "unruly, aggressive, violent" behaviour (official police statement)
  • Delhi Police vehicle vandalized and overturned near Janpath
  • The march was unauthorized from the start, Section 163 BNSS in effect
  • 50+ police personnel injured in the clashes
Narrative B

CJP: "Police Provoked / Pre-Planned It"

  • Truck loaded with stones and a wrecked van documented on video at Jantar Mantar before the march
  • Video footage shows police throwing stones at protesters, not the other way around
  • 5,000+ police, 25 CAPF battalions, electrified barricades deployed before a single protester moved
  • Police had no name tags, preventing identification and accountability
  • CJP explicitly instructed protesters to be peaceful: flags, Constitution, no weapons
  • Gitanjali Angmo pushed, a child injured, stage dismantled by police
  • Mobile internet cut to suppress evidence

Assessment

The state created the conditions for violence.

The stone truck was documented on video before the march started. Video footage shows police throwing stones at protesters, not the reverse. The police buildup was disproportionate and preemptive: 5,000+ personnel, electrified barricades, internet shutdown, all for a student protest. Police wore no name tags, eliminating accountability.

The march was peaceful until the barricade breach attempt at 11:25 AM. No violence was reported from 4 AM to 11:25 AM. The first use of force was police lathi charge after protesters tried to push past barricades.

However, the vehicle overturned near Janpath and 50+ injuries to police personnel suggest some protesters did engage in violence once clashes began. Whether these were genuine CJP supporters or infiltrators cannot be confirmed from available reporting.

The most accurate summary: The state created a pressure cooker, then lit the match.
